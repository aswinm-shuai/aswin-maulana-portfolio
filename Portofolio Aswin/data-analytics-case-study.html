02 — Business Question

In plain words: The analysis asks which menu items sell well and what affects the size of an order.

Background

The dataset records restaurant orders, menu categories, quantities, order totals, dates, and payment methods across 2022 and 2023.

Problem

The restaurant needs a clear view of sales drivers and order patterns to guide menu and sales decisions.

Objectives

Compare categories and items, review sales changes, and test which order details help explain order value.

Key Questions

  • Which categories and items earn the most?
  • How did sales change from 2022 to 2023?
  • How do quantity and category shape order value?
  • Do payment method, weekday, or month change sales?

06 — Recommendations

In plain words: Use the strongest sales patterns to guide menu offers, and keep watching the year-over-year decline.

Menu leadersProtect and promote Pasta Alfredo, Grilled Chicken, and Steak. Test price or placement for Iced Tea and Nachos Grande before deciding on them.
Small ordersBundle cheap, popular items with Main Dishes to lift small orders. Pilot first, because the data cannot measure bundle effects.
Group order valueEncourage larger Main Dish orders, such as group sets. One extra Main Dish adds about 15 to an order, one extra Drink about 2.
Promotion timingDo not plan promotions or staffing around weekday, month, or payment method on this data alone. Differences are small. Keep monitoring the 2023 decline.

07 — Limitations

In plain words: The data shows order patterns, but it does not explain costs or why sales changed.

Limitations

  • Data looks synthetic and very evenly spread.
  • No cost, profit, or time-of-day data.
  • The 2023 decline was not tested statistically.
  • Each order holds one item, so basket analysis was not possible.
  • Models were not tuned and the data was split once, so the top four models are effectively tied.
